History of AT&T Long Lines

AT&T Long Lines was the long-distance network of the Bell System: telephone circuits, and later television and data, spanning the United States. It was built from open wire, coaxial cable, microwave radio, and finally fiber optics, with repeater stations and switching centers in between.

The network was an engineering project on a continental scale. Horn-reflector towers on ridgelines, buried L-carrier cables, and electronic switches in regional centers were all parts of the same plant.

Technology behind Long Lines

Microwave radio relay and coaxial L-carrier carried analog FDM groups across the country with regularly spaced repeaters. Switching evolved from manual toll boards and crossbar to stored-program ESS machines. Fiber optics later replaced most of the analog radio and coaxial plant.

Bell Labs

Bell Telephone Laboratories was the research and development arm of the Bell System. Its researchers produced the transistor, information theory, the silicon solar cell, the CCD, UNIX and C, contributions to the laser, and the Telstar satellite, among many other results. Several Nobel Prizes in Physics have been awarded for work done there.
