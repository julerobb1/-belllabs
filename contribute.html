Contribute

This is a volunteer historical site about AT&T Long Lines and Bell Labs. If you have photographs of stations, documents, corrections, or first-hand knowledge, you can help.

Photographs and documents

Useful contributions include:

  • Photographs of former Long Lines towers, horns, coaxial huts, and operating buildings (with location and date if you know them)
  • Scans of non-confidential Bell System Practices, training notes, or route maps you are allowed to share
  • Corrections to names, dates, or technical descriptions on these pages

Please do not send material that is still proprietary, that you do not have the right to publish, or that was obtained by trespassing. Blur license plates and faces if privacy is a concern.

The gallery upload form on the home page is not connected to a server. To send files, open an issue or pull request on the project’s GitHub repository, or ask in the Discord community linked from the home page.

Editing the site

The site is plain HTML and CSS. There is no framework. To propose a change:

  1. Fork julerobb1/-belllabs.
  2. Edit the relevant .html page. Shared layout lives in css/site.css and js/site.js. Keep new pages in the same header/nav/footer pattern as an existing article.
  3. Use relative links. Do not add trackers or third-party ads.
  4. Open a pull request with a short description of what you changed.

Factual claims about Bell System technology should be checkable against the Bell System Technical Journal, Bell Labs / AT&T historical publications, or other primary sources. Do not invent quotations or traffic figures.

Community

A Discord server for people interested in Long Lines and Bell Labs history is embedded on the home page. That widget is the public join path; this site does not collect email addresses.
