Robert W. Lucky

Robert (Bob) W. Lucky is an electrical engineer who spent his research career at Bell Labs. He is known in communications engineering for adaptive equalization—automatically correcting the distortion of a telephone channel so that high-speed data could be sent over ordinary lines—and later as an executive and writer about technology.

Equalization and data on the telephone network

Voice telephone channels were not designed for digital modems. Their amplitude and delay vary with frequency, which smears pulses. An adaptive equalizer is a filter that learns the channel and undoes that smearing while the data is running. Lucky’s work on automatic equalization, published in the 1960s (including papers in the Bell System Technical Journal and IEEE Transactions), became part of the standard toolkit for voice-band modems and, more generally, for high-speed digital transmission.

He held research and management posts at Bell Labs, including work in the Communications Sciences area, and later wrote columns and books for a general audience, among them Lucky Strikes Again and Silicon Dreams. Those books are his own words; this page does not quote them.

Why he is on this site

Long Lines and the Bell operating companies ran the analog plant; people like Lucky worked on how to push data through it and how to design the next digital plant. His career is a reminder that Bell Labs was not only transistors and UNIX. It was also the unglamorous mathematics of making a noisy, band-limited channel carry bits.
