Preservation efforts

The Long Lines microwave and coaxial plant was a continental engineering work. Most of it is gone from service. Towers have been sold or torn down, horn-reflector antennas scrapped, and coaxial repeaters pulled from manholes. What can still be saved is documentation: photographs, maps, equipment, and the knowledge of people who built and maintained the network.

What this community is doing

This site is one volunteer effort to keep that record in public view. We collect photographs of remaining stations, point to technical literature, and describe how the systems worked. We are not AT&T, we do not own the sites, and we do not encourage trespassing. Many former Long Lines parcels are private property, fenced, or reused by other communications companies.

Sites, maps, and paper

Independent researchers have already mapped thousands of microwave stations and cable huts. The most widely used public map of Long Lines microwave is maintained at long-lines.com (N3TUQ). Historic aerial photography (for example USGS and local assessor imagery) often shows the square fenced compound and the building-plus-tower silhouette even after the horns are gone.

Original engineering is still readable. The Bell System Technical Journal, Bell System Practices, and Western Electric manufacturing information describe TD-2, L-carrier, ESS, and the rest in primary language. Digitized BSTJ issues are available through the Internet Archive. Paper copies turn up in university libraries and in collections of retired employees.

Equipment and buildings

A few museums and private collectors hold KS horn-reflectors, TD/TH radio bays, L-carrier repeaters, and ESS frames. Station buildings—often windowless concrete or brick with louvered plenum walls—are sometimes reused as cell sites, data huts, or storage. Where a tower still carries the original horns, that is now rare and photographically valuable.

If you own a former site or a piece of Western Electric Long Lines hardware and want it documented, use the contribute page. Do not strip active communications sites. Do not enter fenced compounds without permission.
